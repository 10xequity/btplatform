## §2. What shipped this session

**One release: v0.95.0 — §-1b W-D, seeding suggestions.** Built from

### The session's most reusable finding

**A guard's own comment tripped the guard for a fourth time in three sessions — and this time it was
a comment I wrote in the same hour.** `board_suggest.js` explains that it invents no "N Co" region
taxonomy; the test asserting there is no invented "N Co" taxonomy read the file including comments
and went red. The same shape hit a second check in the same file (a comment saying "there is no
'no suggestions' state" tripped the no-empty-state-copy check).

The habit that actually prevents it: **when a guard asserts what code DOES, strip comments at the
top of the file, once, into a named constant, and give the stripper a control in both directions** —
prove the phrase is present in the raw text and absent from the stripped text, and that the code
survived. `board_suggestions.test.mjs` does this with `MOD_CODE`. Asserting over raw source is the
default that keeps failing, and it fails *silently in the other direction* too: a guard that greps
raw source for a rule can pass because a comment mentions the rule.

**Second finding, on fixtures:** three assertions in the first draft of the guard passed or failed
for a reason the test never named, because one fixture person held *two* claims — a champion who had
also placed first at an older event, and a future-event winner who had also placed third in the past.
**A test about one mechanism needs a fixture subject whose only claim is that mechanism.** Two extra
contacts fixed it, and A4b/A4c/A3 then failed honestly and were fixed for real.

### v0.95.0 — §-1b W-D (seeding suggestions: proposals, never rules)

**What the owner settled, and how it changed the build.** Both spec §7 questions were asked once and
answered, and the second answer widened the design:

> **Subs** — *"Subs for tournaments, non issue we wont update as we dont have subs for that usually.
> For leagues yes, and we can count the number of wins they have as subs on their record (and
> divisions played). The team doesnt record doesnt matter if they have subs, but they should be noted
> if they get subs regularly to admin."*

So W-D excludes `is_sub` rows entirely, and the league-side sub record became **roadmap §-1b W-I** —
count wins and divisions played, never losses, flag a team that takes subs regularly.

> **"Won before"** — *"We should rank every team, so depending out of bracket, from pool they should
> count all teams 1-X for each division. Then simply list their placement for divisions. We can award
> a score/number as there is minor overlap between bottom of A and top of BB, similar to ELO score.
> Modidified since this is a team sport. - rank includes Open/AA - A - BB - B - Recreational. I would
> just want their placement within their division and estimated score total then convert into ElO
> number for raning potentially."*

So a team's history is **placement within its division plus a tier-weighted score**: tiers 100 apart
(Open/AA 400 · A 300 · BB 200 · B 100 · Recreational 0, plus the mixed labels the real registration
form uses — `A/AA` 350, `BB/A` 250, `B/BB` 150), placing scored 0–100 inside a tier. **The bottom of A
and the top of BB therefore come out equal by construction**, which is the overlap the owner
described, derived rather than tuned. An unrecognised division label earns **no** tier and is left out
of the strength comparison rather than guessed at. A *persisted* ELO rating is **roadmap §-1b W-H**,
unbuilt on purpose: the owner said "potentially", and it needs a ratings table this release does not
add.

**Built:** `worker/src/board_suggest.js` (read-only, single export) + one key on `loadBoard`'s return
+ a panel on `web/admin-pool-board.html` + `board_suggestions.test.mjs`. **No new route, no
migration.** The board POST already spreads `...board`, so the panel refreshes on every save with no
second request.

Four signals, each in its own `try/catch` returning `[]`: **`spread_winners`** (division winners
bunched in one pool) · **`spread_strength`** (one pool finished higher than another, stated in
*divisions' worth* — the internal score is a unit nobody has been taught and never reaches the
screen) · **`split_repeat`** (two teams in a pool whose players keep sharing rosters, counted once per
shared roster, not once per pair of people) · **`spread_area`** (one city bunched in one pool; captain
city, literal stored string, **no region taxonomy**, so "Fort Collins" and "Ft Collins" stay two
groups and the signal understates).

**The three product rules, each asserted:** the 6–11 preference suppresses a *suggestion* and never a
save · an unsatisfiable suggestion is dropped **in silence**, with no "not enough history" line
anywhere · **no sentence names a person**, though every signal resolves identity through people.

**The panel** sits between `#pbBoard` and `.pb-workspace` — outside `#pbBoard`, which `render()`
rewrites and `fail()`/`orgEmptyState()` replace wholesale. Drawn from `ingest()` only, never from
`render()` or `wire()`, with one delegated listener on a static node, because `wire()` already stacks
drag handlers on the never-recreated `#pbWork` (§-1c D-6). Rows carry **Show me** (rings the teams,
clears itself after 4s) and **Dismiss**. Visually it is the board's own furniture one register
quieter, with a gold left edge as a *rule* — never gold ink.

**The guard (+22, 1290 → 1312).** The fixture forces all four kinds, because a no-write assertion over
a payload that proposed nothing is vacuous. `rank` is written to **disagree** with pool play wherever
the two can be told apart, so a module reading `rank` fails here: a team holding `rank=1` that placed
last and lost its final must not be credited, while a team that placed third and won a *scored* final
must be. Ties credit nobody; a **drawn** final credits nobody (without `score_a <> score_b` a tie
crowns `team_b` through the `ELSE` branch); a future-dated `in_progress` event is not history. The
no-write assertion **records every prepared statement**, so a no-op `UPDATE teams SET pool_id=pool_id`
is caught — NC-3 proves that detector fires by inserting exactly that into a copy of the real module.
NC-1 renames a real column and asserts the two history signals go silent **while the signal that
never touched that column still answers**, which is the per-signal isolation being demonstrated rather
than asserted. A recorded limit: a **whitespace-padded** email does not resolve to a contact, because
wrapping the `contacts` side of the join in `trim()` would defeat `idx_contacts_org_email` for every
lookup — A6b pins that trade so it cannot be reversed in one direction only.

**Live-data reality check, measured this session:** live D1 has exactly **one** past event (Spring
Slam, 4 teams, **zero divisions**) and five future-dated ones. So on real data today the tier-weighted
strength signal produces **nothing** — there is no past event with a division to read. That is correct
behaviour, not a defect, and it is why the guard's fixture had to manufacture the history.
