## §1. The owner's words

> "that singleplayer is usually a league format for a single entry - queens or kings style tournament,
> where they are paired with another person - draft format. This is just a format to play. they will
> play with everyone then change the next round where the top players on that net move to the next
> completing 4 to a net. They will enter scores, which tally and then ranked and seeded. They then
> fill the next net based on number of nets - determine number of players going up."

And, asked whether the same two people may be paired again in a later round:

> "yes they can repeat - idealy not if possible, but it can happen, not a fixed position."

## §2. What the format is

**Individuals enter, not teams.** One person registers. There is no team record for them to belong to.

**Four to a net.** Each round, four players occupy a net and play as two pairs. Four players make
exactly three distinct pairings:

| Game | Pair | Against |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A + B | C + D |
| 2 | A + C | B + D |
| 3 | A + D | B + C |

So a round on one net is three games and every player partners each of the other three exactly once.

**A net of FIVE is a real shape, not a degraded four** — see §6 Q2. Five players play **five** games in
which all ten pairs partner exactly once and every player sits out exactly one game. C(5,2) = 10, and
5 games × 2 pairs = 10, so a perfect rotation exists at five. No scaling, no scoring adjustment.

**Points are individual.** A player's score is what their pair scored while they were in it, across
every game they played. The owner said scores "tally and then ranked and seeded" — a running total
rather than a win count.

**Movement between rounds.** The top `move_up` players on a net move UP one net and the bottom
`move_up` move DOWN one. Net 1 is the top and has nowhere higher; the bottom net has nowhere lower.
`move_up` is **a number the director sets per session** — see §6 Q1.

**Partner repeats are a soft constraint** — and much softer than v1.0 assumed. See §7 finding 1.

## §3. What this needs that does not exist — BUILT as migration 0040

`teams` is the unit of play everywhere else and a KOTC pairing lasts one game. Throwaway two-person
team rows would pollute `teams`, `standings`, and every report that reads them.

**Migration 0040, applied to live D1 2026-08-03**, ledger row read back. `sqlite_master` was read
first (F-41): zero `kotc*` tables existed.

```
kotc_sessions   id, org_id, event_id, name, players_per_net (4), move_up (1), points_to (21),
                rounds_planned, status, created/updated/deleted
kotc_rounds     id, org_id, session_id, round_no, created/updated/deleted
kotc_slots      id, org_id, round_id, net_no, seat, contact_id     -- seat is 0..3, or 0..4 on a five
kotc_games      id, org_id, round_id, net_no, game_no,
                a1/a2/b1/b2_contact_id, score_a, score_b, points_to, created/updated/deleted
```

Changed from v1.0's proposal:

- **`games_per_round` dropped.** It is 3 or 5 depending on a net's size — per-net and derived, never a
  session-wide setting.
- **No `cap` column.** "No cap" was the owner's choice (§6 Q3); a nullable cap invites a default that
  quietly reinstates one.
- **`move_up` defaults to 1** and no formula computes it anywhere.

**Standings are DERIVED from `kotc_games`, never stored.** The F-26 lesson the passes module already
paid for: a stored counter and the games it came from will disagree, and the counter is the one people
will have been reading.

**The four players are stored ON the game**, not resolved through `kotc_slots`. A director moves people
between nets on the day; a game that resolved its players through the seating would silently change who
played it every time somebody was dragged.

## §4. The engine — BUILT, `worker/src/kotc.js`, 29 tests

All pure: no database, no request, no clock.

| Function | What it does | State |
|---|---|---|
| `rotation(size)` | The 3 games of a net of four, or the 5 of a net of five, as seat indices | ✅ |
| `gamesPerNet(size)` | 3 or 5. Derived, never stored | ✅ |
| `netPlan(count)` | Player count → net sizes. Refuses 6, 7, 11 with two counts that would work | ✅ |
| `seedRound(ranked)` | Deals players into nets, best on net 1 | ✅ |
| `gamesForRound(nets)` | Every fixture, players named on the game | ✅ |
| `tally(games)` | Per-player points, conceded, margin, wins, losses, games played | ✅ |
| `rankPlayers(rows)` | Points → wins → margin → contact id | ✅ |
| `partnerHistory(games)` | Counts repeats; reporting, not steering (§7) | ✅ |
| `nextRound(prev, tally, {moveUp})` | The next net assignment, sizes preserved | ✅ |

## §5. Where it surfaces — UNBUILT

- **Admin:** a board per round — nets down the page, four (or five) seats each, drag a player between
  nets. The pool board is the closest model, but the tile is a PERSON and the zone is a NET.
- **Scoring:** three (or five) games per net per round. The captain-link pattern does not fit — there
  is no team and no captain. Likeliest answer is **one link per NET**, entered by whoever is nearest
  the pole. Not yet asked.
- **Public:** the live board groups by division and pool; a KOTC session is a third shape, and an
  **individual leaderboard** is what spectators actually want.

## §6. The five questions — ALL ANSWERED, owner 2026-08-03

**Q1. How many players move up? → "Director sets it each session."**
The owner's own description held two rules that agree only at some net counts: *"We take the top 8
scores amongst nets, usually its 1 per net for equity. But with fewer nets, we may take more than 1."*
Four candidate formulas were offered, including two that reproduce "top 8" exactly (2 per net at four
nets → 8; 1 per net at eight nets → 8). He declined all four and chose the director setting it.
**So no formula is encoded anywhere.** `move_up` is a column, default **1** (his "usually 1 per net for
equity"), clamped to at most half a net, and a test asserts the engine never derives it from the net
count. [INTERPRETATION] The default of 1 rather than 2 is a reading of "usually 1 per net"; a director
who wants 2 types 2.

**Q2. A field that is not a multiple of four? → Leftovers JOIN existing nets, making nets of five.**
*"we would fill each person to join an existing net and do a 5 team rotation rotating pairs. However,
this should not happen where people drop, we would go in with even numbers."* So 14 players is
**4 / 5 / 5**, not v1.0's proposed 4 / 4 / 4 / 2 — a net of two is not a net. 6, 7 and 11 players are
the only counts above 4 that cannot be made of fours and fives; those are refused, naming the two
nearest counts that work. [INTERPRETATION] The fives sit on the BOTTOM nets — the owner said only
"join an existing net". Net 1 decides the night and a three-game net is the cleaner comparison for the
players contesting it.

**Q3. Points-to per game? → First to 21, no cap, per session, director can change it.**
Chosen over the pool rule of 25 (cap 27): a round is three games on one net, and a net still playing
while three others have finished is what stalls the night.

**Q4. Ranking tiebreak? → "Wins, then point difference."**
Total points stays primary — the owner's own "scores, which tally and then ranked and seeded" — with
wins and then margin under it, then contact id so the order is never random. Head-to-head is
deliberately impossible here: partnerships change every game, so two players have usually been on the
same side as often as against.

**Q5. Does the 8-game floor apply? → "No — this format sets its own length."**
`MIN_GAMES_PER_TEAM = 8` is a pool-play promise about a tournament day. A KOTC league night finishes
when it finishes. No minimum-rounds column, no check, and a test asserts the pool floor has not crept
in. (Four rounds clears 8 games incidentally — 12 — but nothing enforces it.)

## §7. Two findings against v1.0 of this document

**1. v1.0 §4 item 5 asked for something that cannot exist.** It wanted `partnerHistory` "so
`nextRound` can prefer a fresh pairing when it has a free choice". **It never has a free choice.** A
net plays *all* its pairings — three at four players, ten at five. The only freedom is the ORDER of
the games, which does not change who partners whom.

So a partner repeat is decided entirely by **who shares a net**, and that is decided by the scores.
`partnerHistory` is therefore reporting, plus a tie-break of last resort when two level players compete
for one place. Still exactly what the owner asked for — "not a fixed position" — but the honest
mechanism is far smaller than v1.0 assumed, and an optimiser with nothing to optimise would have been
theatre. Recorded in `kotc.js`'s header and asserted by a test so it cannot be quietly deleted.

**2. The net-of-five rotation is easy to get wrong in a way that looks right.** Pairing the four
non-sitting players as (k+1, k+2) against (k+3, k+4) — the arrangement most people write first — still
gives five games, everybody still plays four, everybody still sits out once. It forms five distinct
pairs twice each instead of ten once each, and quietly stops being fair. The correct construction is
(k+1, k+4) against (k+2, k+3). A negative control builds the wrong one and proves only the pair count
distinguishes them.

## §8. What is left

1. **Routes** — `kotcRoutes` / `wireKotc` in their own module, mounted in `index.js`. The engine ships
   unreachable on purpose and a ratchet test in `kotc.test.mjs` goes red the moment they are wired;
   delete that test then and replace it with the usual dispatch-chain assertion (standards §6.5).
2. **The admin board** — nets down the page, drag a person between them. Load the design roster first
   (`skill-library:frontend-design` + `skill-library:web-design-guidelines`; `/emil-design-eng` is not
   installed here). Generate the page only from one that passes `page_structure.test.mjs`.
3. **Scoring** — one link per net is the likely shape. **Ask the owner** before building it; it is not
   among the five answered above.
4. **Public leaderboard** — individual, on the existing live board as a third shape.
