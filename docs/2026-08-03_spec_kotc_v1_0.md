## §1. The owner's words

> "that singleplayer is usually a league format for a single entry - queens or kings style tournament,
> where they are paired with another person - draft format. This is just a format to play. they will
> play with everyone then change the next round where the top players on that net move to the next
> completing 4 to a net. They will enter scores, which tally and then ranked and seeded. They then
> fill the next net based on number of nets - determine number of players going up."

And, asked whether the same two people may be paired again in a later round:

> "yes they can repeat - idealy not if possible, but it can happen, not a fixed position."

---

## §2. What the format is

**Individuals enter, not teams.** One person registers. There is no team record for them to belong to.

**Four to a net.** Each round, four players occupy a net and play as two pairs.

**Everyone plays with everyone on their net.** Four players make exactly three distinct pairings:

| Game | Pair | Against |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A + B | C + D |
| 2 | A + C | B + D |
| 3 | A + D | B + C |

So a round on one net is three games and every player partners each of the other three exactly once.

**Points are individual.** A player's score is what their pair scored while they were in it, across all
three games. Wins matter, and so does margin — the owner said scores "tally and then ranked and
seeded", which is a running total rather than a win count.

**Movement between rounds.** The top players on a net move UP to the next net; the rest move down or
stay. How many move is a function of how many nets there are — the owner's phrase is "fill the next
net based on number of nets - determine number of players going up". Net 1 is the top.

**Partner repeats are a soft constraint.** Avoid re-pairing two people who have already partnered,
but never fail or refuse a round over it. Same shape as the existing repeat-opponent handling in
`formats.js`: minimise, count, report, do not block.

## §3. What this needs that does not exist

Nothing in the schema can hold this. `teams` is the unit of play everywhere else, and a KOTC pairing
lasts one game. Creating throwaway two-person team rows would pollute `teams`, `standings`, and every
report that reads them.

**Proposed migration 0040** — verify with `sqlite_master` first (F-41), these names do not exist as of
ledger 0039:

```
kotc_sessions   id, org_id, event_id, name, players_per_net (default 4), games_per_round (default 3),
                move_up, move_down, rounds_planned, status, created/updated/deleted
kotc_rounds     id, org_id, session_id, round_no, created/updated/deleted
kotc_slots      id, org_id, round_id, net_no, seat, contact_id     -- who is on which net this round
kotc_games      id, org_id, round_id, net_no, game_no,
                a1_contact_id, a2_contact_id, b1_contact_id, b2_contact_id,
                score_a, score_b, points_to, created/updated/deleted
```

**Standings are DERIVED from `kotc_games`, never stored.** This is the F-26 lesson the passes module
already learned: a stored per-player counter and the games it came from will disagree, and the counter
is the one people will have been reading.

## §4. The engine, as pure functions

Worth building in this order, because each is testable without the ones after it:

1. `pairings(four)` → the three pairings above. Trivial, and the anchor everything else assumes.
2. `netCount(playerCount, playersPerNet)` → how many nets, and what to do with a remainder that is not
   a multiple of four. **Open question, §6.**
3. `tally(games)` → per-player points, wins, and margin. Derived, one definition.
4. `nextRound(previousRound, tally, { moveUp })` → the new net assignment. Top `moveUp` on each net go
   up one, bottom `moveUp` go down one, net 1 and the last net absorb their own edges.
5. `partnerHistory(rounds)` → who has already played with whom, so `nextRound` can prefer a fresh
   pairing when it has a free choice. Soft only.

## §5. Where it surfaces

- **Admin:** a board per round — nets down the page, four seats each, drag a player between nets. The
  existing pool board is the closest model but the tile is a PERSON and the zone is a NET.
- **Scoring:** three games per net per round. The captain-link pattern does not fit — there is no team
  and no captain. Likeliest answer is one link per NET, entered by whoever is nearest the pole.
- **Public:** the live board already groups by division/pool; a KOTC session is a third shape, and an
  individual leaderboard is the thing spectators actually want.

## §6. Open questions — ask before building these parts

1. **How many players move up?** The owner said it depends on the number of nets, but not the formula.
   Most common in the wild is the top 2 of 4 up and the bottom 2 down. Confirm.
2. **A field that is not a multiple of four.** 14 players is three nets of four and one of two. Does
   the short net play a different rotation, or does somebody sit out a round?
3. **Points-to per game.** KOTC games are usually short and capped by time, not by points. Is it a
   fixed target, or first-to-N with no cap?
4. **Ranking tiebreak.** Total points, then what — head-to-head is meaningless when partnerships
   change every game.
5. **Does the 8-game floor apply?** `MIN_GAMES_PER_TEAM = 8` is a pool-play rule. A KOTC round is 3
   games, so a night needs at least 3 rounds to clear the floor. Confirm whether the floor is meant to
   reach this format at all.
